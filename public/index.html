Featured case study

Orra Ring.The full UX plan behind a product launch.

A smart ring I made up to plan a full launch: who it's for, what the page must answer, how the 3D story moves and what happens after the click.

Read the case study Open the live page ↗
  1. 01

    One promise for two people

    I started with two proto-personas: a tired professional who wants better sleep without tracking homework, and a runner who needs to know when to rest. Both get the same promise, right in the hero: Rest, measured.

  2. 02

    Every section answers one question

    I listed what people ask before buying, like "will I even notice it on my finger?", and gave each question its own section, in the order it comes up. Material answers comfort: 4 g and 2.4 mm thin.

  3. 03

    Motion that explains the product

    The 3D ring follows a motion script I wrote before building. It turns edge-on for material, faces you for the sensors and draws a charge arc for the battery. Each pose answers the claim beside it.

  4. 04

    A plan before the email

    A plain waitlist only asks. Here two quick questions build a personal sleep plan, and only then does the page ask for an email. Both answers turn into CRM segments.

  5. 05

    The click keeps working

    Choosing a finish, opening a section, finishing the quiz: each one is a named event. I planned ten of them, plus the lifecycle stages and the emails each segment gets after sign-up.

Meet Vk.

I'm Victor, but everyone calls me Vk. I started in game design, and it still shapes how I work: I look for the moment someone hesitates and ask what would make the next step feel obvious, maybe even fun.

I led a team of four designers across 13+ products in education, health and construction. From 2025 to 2026 I owned the gamification of three regulated consumer brands, from research to code, and led the 4Play redesign that was later presented as a reference in Europe, Cyprus and Latin America.
